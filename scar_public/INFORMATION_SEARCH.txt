Resource-constrained contingent planning: information cost and search
Research checkpoint: 2026-10-16. Offline finite models only; NOT a novelty claim.

1. Implemented and deliberately restricted scope

All models are finite, deterministic, fixed during a solve, and share actions and
positive action costs. The existing planner searches observation-based strong
policies: one policy must complete the goal in every live world while respecting
allowed(state). The action-count horizon includes failed actions and observations.
Completion is observable. Candidate family coverage and goal translation are
assumptions, not conclusions. No success exemptions are introduced in this study.

The baseline is the existing exact-certificate symbolic/resource LP bound:
    h_LP(B,H) = max_(m,x in B) L_m(x,H).
Its search implementation is unchanged. New subclasses override only the initial
lower bound. No new criterion has been enabled in V6/V8 or run on a full game.

2. Diagnostic pair relaxation

Project to two worlds. Until their observations differ, apply the SAME action to
both, using their actual resource transitions. After an observation split, either
use the single-world LP tails (pair_lp) or exact known-world costs (pair_exact).
Recursion stops at a shared goal, a violated constraint, or the finite horizon.
Maximize completed pair values, and retain h_LP. Exact singleton costs are a
separate baseline to distinguish better per-world planning from information cost.

Admissibility follows by restricting any full policy to each pair, then relaxing
its post-split continuations. A finite policy that works for the full belief also
works for every projection; its worst cost cannot be below the projected optimum.
LP tails can only decrease that optimum. Positive infinity from a COMPLETED pair
calculation is therefore a valid no-strong-policy certificate. Interrupted minima
over actions are not lower bounds; the implementation discards them and falls
back to finished projections / certified LP bounds. A pair DP node limit is not
a total wall-clock or LP-work limit and does not budget the capacity relaxation.

Two-model projection is a familiar relaxation principle, not an original family
claim. It can be as hard as the whole problem when there are only two worlds.
All pair preprocessing and dynamic-programming work is included in timings.

3. Candidate resource-indexed policy coverage relaxation

The second criterion bounds how many different candidate models a single policy
could complete, rather than requiring identification of the true model.

Additional assumptions for this implementation:
  (a) unit action costs (otherwise it declines and the baseline remains);
  (b) all worlds in a queried nonterminal belief have the same resource vector r;
  (c) for a given r, action a, and nonterminal observation o, all represented
      transitions yielding o have one common successor resource vector r'(r,a,o);
  (d) all transitions used by admissible executions within H are represented.
Resource gains are allowed if the represented state space remains finite. The
application of interest has irreversible resources, but the proof need not assume
monotone depletion. Hidden/ambiguous resource observations disable this criterion.

Let M be the complete compiled candidate family. For each r and a, discard source
states already at the goal or violating K, and discard transitions violating K.
Over ALL remaining symbolic states with resource r, define:
  G(r,a)   = model IDs that can immediately reach the goal using a;
  N(r,a,o) = model IDs that can produce observation o in a non-goal successor.
Symbolic-state consistency is relaxed: different branches may use mutually
inconsistent symbolic witnesses. G and N sets may overlap, and the same model may
be counted in several branches. This only makes the coverage upper bound larger.

Let C(r,t) be defined by:
  C(r,0) = 0;
  C(r,t) = min(|M|,
               max_a [ |G(r,a)|
                       + sum_o min(|N(r,a,o)|, C(r'(r,a,o),t-1)) ]).
An absent action channel contributes zero. C is an UPPER bound on the number of
distinct models that can be completed safely within t actions, allowing arbitrary
non-goal symbolic starting states with resource r. It is not a count of hypotheses
eliminated, a success probability, or a feasible-policy construction.

For a nonterminal belief B with common r and n distinct live model IDs:
  h_C(B,H) = min { t in {1,...,H} : C(r,t) >= n }, with min(empty)=infinity;
  h_new(B,H) = max(h_LP(B,H), h_C(B,H)).
Mixed resources or mixed goal/non-goal worlds return zero from h_C. The existing
planner still handles K, goals, and the LP baseline. Successful benchmark policies
are generated by full AND/OR search, not by the capacity table.

4. Correctness, strict improvement, and limitations

Theorem 1 (coverage upper bound).
Under the assumptions above, a depth-at-most-t observable policy, starting from
non-goal states with common resource r, cannot safely complete more than C(r,t)
distinct model IDs.

Proof by induction on t. For t=0 no non-goal world completes. At t>0, fix the first
action a. Models completing immediately belong to G(r,a). Every other successfully
completed model has a unique actual nonterminal observation o and belongs to
N(r,a,o). Assumption (c) gives resource r' throughout that observed branch. By the
induction hypothesis its continuation completes at most C(r',t-1) distinct models,
and at most |N(r,a,o)|. Sum over observations and add immediate completions. This
may double count model IDs across relaxed possibilities; double counting increases
an upper bound and is harmless. Maximize over a and cap by the total family size.
Observable goals allow completed branches to stop without further action. QED.

Corollary 1 (admissibility and sound rejection).
A strong policy must complete all n live model IDs. If its worst length is t,
Theorem 1 implies C(r,t)>=n, hence h_C <= t. Therefore h_new never exceeds the
optimal worst cost and dominates h_LP. If C(r,H)<n, no strong policy exists within
H. The converse is FALSE: C is only an optimistic coverage relaxation.

Proposition 2 (strict improvement and a higher-order conflict).
Consider n hidden recipe calibrations, actions make_i, r units of material, and
no other observations/actions. make_i consumes one unit; it succeeds exactly in
model i, otherwise the task remains unfinished and the failure is observed.
Each known model has cost one. When r,H>=2, every pair has cost two. However a
full policy requires n units and n actions in the worst case. Our recurrence is
    C(r,t) = min(n,r,t),
so h_C=n when r,H>=n, and infinity otherwise. This follows by induction from one
immediate success and one remaining failure branch. With n=3,r=2,H=3, every pair
is feasible but the full task is not; h_LP=1, h_pair=2, h_C=infinity. For arbitrary
n the gap over the per-world bound grows to n. This is a separation from THESE
baselines, not from every prior belief-space heuristic.

Proposition 3 (binary information with consumable tests).
Let n=2^k calibrated recipes be distinguished by k binary tests. Each test and
each final fabrication consumes one material unit. A wrong fabrication irreversibly
violates K. Then for r,t>=1 the coverage relaxation is
    C(r,t) = min(n, 2^(min(r,t)-1)),
with zero capacity if r=0 or t=0. The required worst length is k+1, including the
final fabrication; h_C certifies infeasibility when materials or time are smaller.
The recurrence uses two continuation branches for a test and one immediate goal
model for fabrication. Balanced bit queries attain the bound. Per-world cost is
one and pair cost is two. No generic additive 'testing fee' is imposed.

Productive-information countercheck. If one action completes all n models while
revealing the calibration, |G(r,a)|=n. Then h_C=1. Information and progress are
not charged twice, and identification is never required when shared completion
is available.

Ordering limitation. A safe scan may need d ordered preparation actions. Since
symbolic progress is relaxed, channels from a later preparation stage can be used
too early in C. In our delayed-scan examples h_C can be 2 while the true optimum
and pair bound are d+1. Thus capacity and pair bounds do NOT dominate each other.

Computational cost. Given the already compiled finite transition graph, channel
construction is linear in represented transitions plus storage for distinct
model memberships. With R distinct resource vectors and E aggregate action and
observation channels, dynamic programming costs O(H*(E+R)) arithmetic operations and O(HR)
cache entries, ignoring tuple/set processing. It does not enumerate beliefs or
model pairs. This is polynomial in the EXPLICIT compiled graph, not necessarily
in a compact PDDL/LLM description; graph compilation can itself be exponential.

5. Evaluation protocol and what it establishes

Six variants use the SAME finite models and unchanged exact AND/OR search:
  lp, single, pair_lp, pair_exact, capacity_lp, capacity_single.
The single variant computes exact per-world costs; capacity_single isolates the
coverage effect without LP solver overhead. Every result is checked against an
independent unpruned minimax recursion. Every finite returned policy is replayed
in every initial world. Tests include bounded-computation fallback, weighted-cost
fallback, productive information, unsafe material probes, and hidden-resource
abstention. Generated validation additionally checks all reachable belief/horizon
pairs and all their nonempty subsets in 64 deterministic small transition systems.

The 66-instance grid has five controlled families: delayed observations, consumptive
probes, material-limited retries, binary observations, and productive information.
Parameters are enumerated, not randomized. Three timing repetitions use fresh
planner/LP/DP caches with rotated method order after one SciPy warmup. Reported
totals sum per-instance median times. They include bound construction/preprocessing,
root bounds and exact search. Common domain compilation and independent oracle
time are reported separately. Policy verification is outside timed search equally
for every method. Tiny absolute times are engineering evidence, not statistical
proof of robust speedups. No confidence intervals or significance claim is made.

Read results/2026-10-16-information-bound/analysis.json and validation.json for
machine-readable results and source hashes. The result is conditional support
for a stronger and inexpensive criterion in these finite controlled problems.
It does not establish benchmark representativeness, general scaling, novelty over
all related work, LLM grounding reliability, or improved 300-step game scores.

6. Positioning and next falsifiable questions

Related foundations that MUST be acknowledged:
  Pommerening et al., LP-based Heuristics for Cost-optimal Planning, ICAPS 2014:
  https://ai.dmi.unibas.ch/papers/pommerening-et-al-icaps2014.pdf
  Bryce et al., Planning Graph Heuristics for Belief Space Search, JAIR 2006:
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.1711
  Shani, Heuristics for Partially Observable Stochastic Contingent Planning,
  ECAI 2024: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05870

Known ideas include projection relaxations, information/decision-tree counting,
belief-space heuristics, and accounting for sensing costs. The precise candidate
to investigate further is the resource-indexed achievable-model-coverage relaxation
with immediate goal coverage and observation-channel capacity. We have NOT yet
proved it is absent from existing literature or compared with all relevant planners.

Next: compare against relevant information-aware planning heuristics, extend
controlled instances to multiple resources, routes and upstream production, and
measure channel-construction cost when states have rich observations. First test
where resource/observation abstraction loses too much information; do not simply
add more modules or enable the method globally in V6. A useful extension must keep
a sound upper coverage bound and demonstrate gains beyond these hand-built families.
