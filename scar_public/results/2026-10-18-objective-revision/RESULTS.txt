Requirement-bound verification and trial revision, 2026-10-18

Conclusion
The implemented repair arm distinguishes action execution from the condition
the trial was supposed to establish, and carries unresolved outcomes into a
bounded revision of the same requirement. It no longer counts arrival as a
successful material search. Recent inadequate-source evidence can reject a
paraphrased repeat. The final full-game scores are 17, 5, 10, 13: one win and
three ties against both the frozen core checked policy and the previous h2 arm.
This is a small development result, not stable general improvement. Reliable
generation of an effective alternative after a failed trial remains unproven.
The arm remains opt-in.

What changed
The requirement target/count is compiled from a pre-proposal public catalog,
separately from LLM-authored step tests. Source, acquisition, access and report
outcomes have different meanings. A source observation can select a pickup;
only actual inventory evidence resolves an acquisition requirement. Plans
ending at unrelated destinations/items cannot claim to test the fixed target.
Failed/unknown trials schedule one bounded revision with the same requirement
and actual observation, without waiting for another loop. Source-specific
evidence is revocable; unknown visibility is not absence. Missing structured
plans receive the existing single correction opportunity. Of the same 12
proposal calls, two are reserved for actual trial feedback. No new entity-
specific resource rule, action family or irreversible-action veto was added.

Frozen development iterations
o1: first requirement-bound verifier and revision ledger. Native Metropolis
failures triggered six revision events; three selected programs and one changed
the actor's action, but none fulfilled the checked requirement. Three successful
execution milestones were correctly distinguished from false objectives.
However, the admitted access programs could end at unrelated locations.
o2: reject such structural endpoint mismatches; also correct missing structured
hypotheses and recognize full source names through their public exit names.
o3 (final): reserve two calls for actual trial feedback. The selected legacy
library prefix had already exhausted its 12-call budget. This explains why
adding feedback alone cannot ensure another proposal is available.
All three source snapshots and all results are retained. o3 reused exact o2
API responses and made zero new API requests in its four full-game runs. This
is a regression replay after a budget-policy edit, NOT an independent repeat.

Positive native chain
In final Mark-r0, decision 58 replaces enter-library with pickup-key. The
selected hypothesis predicts the armory as a copper source. Decision 59 enters
the armory; the next observation actually contains copper. Decision 60 selects
a checked pickup, and decision 61 records the material in hand. Arrival,
source observation, and acquisition are logged separately. The follow-up
actions agree with the ordinary actor; they are not additional overrides.
The copper-sword task completes at decision 63 versus checked 65 and h2 54.
Final completion is 268, slower than h2's 228. This demonstrates a correct
verification chain, not superiority of all its components or faster completion.

Preserving useful behavior
The final Metropolis-r1 trajectory exactly follows the checked policy for all
300 actions, scoring 10 rather than h2's 8. At the shared decision-71 state,
the old proposed diversion ends at the antechamber while the selected access
requirement is the alley. The new gate rejects that mismatch. This is evidence
of avoiding an inadmissible substitution, not discovering a superior route.
Mark-r1 and Metropolis-7537 also have zero overrides and exactly match checked
throughout. Their scores cannot be credited to active new repair behavior.

Remaining scope gaps
1. Requirement coverage is incomplete. Final Mark-r1 has 21 triggered decisions
   without a computable requirement; the fallback continues the ordinary actor.
   Public task selection and upstream material methods remain bottlenecks.
2. A failed entrance is an attempted-method subproblem, not a globally necessary
   destination. Strict endpoint checks can miss a valid method that bypasses it.
3. Native o3 runs contain zero unsuccessful repair trials followed by revision.
   There is no native o3 evidence that the revised feedback mechanism finds a
   successful remedy. Tests and the selected negative probe are not substitutes.
4. Ordinary craft/disassembly decisions remain outside the triggered repair
   supervision. Only pickup/navigation/inspection/dialogue plans are supported.
5. Recent-source deferral is a scoped heuristic with 24-action expiry, not a
   sound impossibility theorem in a dynamic world. Dialogue relevance is also
   heuristic. No whole-game success, safety or optimality guarantee is claimed.
