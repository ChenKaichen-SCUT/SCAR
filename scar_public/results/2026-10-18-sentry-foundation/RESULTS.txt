Sentry on the same AgentOdyssey ReAct/LongContext actor, 2026-10-18

Question and scope
The direct improvement target is author Sentry. All three methods use the same
clean AgentOdyssey ReAct/LongContext prompt, memory and audited model transport.
The old SCAR actor and its notebooks, gates and repair pipeline are not used.
The implemented change is ONLY bounded access to actual historical outcomes
for the Sentry detector and verifier. It is NOT the proposed persistent-blocker
mechanism, and this study cannot establish that larger mechanism's usefulness.

Sentry already diagnoses failures, routes repair advice, verifies recovery
after H=10 actions, and updates its playbook after verified soft recovery.
Its verifier requires task-relevant, observation-grounded progress, not merely
any executable action. The source detaches a soft event at H even when unresolved;
only resolved episodes update the playbook. This motivates studying persistent
unresolved cases, but does not prove that event retirement caused low scores.
Its recent diagnosis window contains W=5 cycles. A historical claim can be
misjudged when its supporting observation is older than that window.

Frozen experiment
One new environment draw per familiar game, shared by the three arms. No chosen
world seed; record the drawn world and Python hash seeds. Run every arm to 300
actions or game termination. Share only identical step-specific model requests
to couple the common prefix and avoid duplicate charges. Identical actions from
identical states are checked by snapshot replay. Subsequent different actions
are different trajectories; this is not a matched-state causal effect estimate.
No tuning or replacement world after seeing results. Per-world initial receipts
are retained in the archive. No full-game failure or unfavorable outcome omitted.

Interpretation
At300 actions, evidence ties original Sentry on Mark (16 vs16), loses on Remnant
(0 vs2), and loses on Metropolis (4 vs8). Mark reaches16 earlier (cycle235 vs254),
but this local speed advantage does not establish an endpoint improvement.
All9 runs remain unpassed. On Metropolis, evidence has MORE self-verified
recoveries (14 vs9) yet LOWER quest score (4 vs8), illustrating why monitor
judgments cannot substitute for task performance. These observations do not
identify a population effect from single trajectories.
Do not pool raw quest points across games with different scales. This is one
draw per familiar game and no repetitions, so neither statistical significance
nor stable improvement follows. Compare the per-game table directly; do not
select the best variant separately in each game. The pilot does not justify
promoting historical retrieval to the default or a paper performance claim.
The algorithm remains frozen; new design work must identify a specific causal
gap rather than tune this retrieval to the observed worlds.

Historical diagnostic: important negative result
All 53 original soft-rescue windows from two selected older development runs
were judged with added historical evidence (26 Mark, 27 Metropolis). Their
original responses were exact cache hits: no paid original-baseline reruns.
These OLD traces used the previous SCAR-prompt actor, not the clean new actor;
their judgments cannot be combined with the new full-game baseline scores.

Mark's original cycle62 diagnosis calls earlier copper acquisition and sword
crafting fabricated. Actual old cycles45/47 show those events. The same diagnosis
also correctly detects a current storage failure; the entire intervention is
not a false positive. The added packet selected cycles31,40,44,45,56,57 and missed
the decisive craft at47. The new detector acknowledges copper acquisition but
still calls crafting unsupported/fabricated. The key error therefore REMAINS.
Ordinary top-K historical retrieval did not reliably supply the evidence needed
to test this claim. We did not retune retrieval to force this one record in.
Correctly detecting some other failures is not proof of successful repair.

The selected original runs contain3/25 and7/26 Sentry-verified recoveries. These
are its self-reports on development runs, not a general Sentry success rate.
All 53 paired judgments and retrieved literal observations are saved, including
changed allegations and failures; there is no fabricated aggregate accuracy.

Concrete unresolved case on the new clean actor
The post-hoc illustration case_metro_capacity.json preserves original Sentry's
cycles226-236. Sentry correctly detects repeated dropping/picking among three
marble_mace ingredients with only two hand slots. The actor changes its proposed
recipe, travels, then cycles among iron_bar and gavel_splint for another recipe
listing three input units, still manipulating two hand slots without attempting
the craft in this window. This does NOT prove that all ingredients must be held:
material accessibility is a hypothesis to check. Sentry's verifier correctly
calls the continuing pickup/drop loop unresolved. Switching recipe changed the
surface behavior without resolving or testing the suspected capacity obstacle.
This is an example of the intended research target;
it is not a causal proof that persistent case tracking will solve it. Sentry
already gives relevant advice and detects the continuing loop in this example.

Next mechanism to test, NOT implemented here
Retain an unresolved case tied to the task condition, current explanation,
attempted repair, expected distinguishing observation, actual result and the
scope under which that result remains applicable. On a failed repair, revise
the explanation or next operation; do not silently retire the unresolved case.
Permit uncertain but grounded proposals. Lack of proof is not automatic rejection.
Use the specific claim to locate its supporting/counterevidence, and preserve
unknown when the evidence packet is incomplete. Close the case only on an
observation that addresses the original condition, not just absence of another
error. Preserve ordinary behavior when there is no supported blocker.
The first comparison should isolate this unresolved-case state from original
Sentry on the SAME actor. Further ablations should separate memory alone from
outcome-dependent plan revision. These are hypotheses, not new guarantees.
