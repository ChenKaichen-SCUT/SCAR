Task-linked compact method repair: bounded development batch, 2026-10-18

Main conclusion
The final opt-in policy can turn the early locked-source situation into an
executed acquisition -> craft -> hold chain in two selected real Mark states.
The old p2 arm makes no such output progress in their eight-action windows.
The tests expose and fix two interface mistakes: treating access as a competing
endpoint instead of a means of acquisition, and reserving a recipe's inputs
twice for two exact aliases of the same task. A third fix cancels an access-only
commitment when the ordinary actor changes to a non-urgent non-navigation action.
These are real mechanism and local behavioral improvements. They do not yet
establish stable whole-game gains; the resource-depletion case is still unsolved.

What the final mechanism does
The public task remains the root. Observed recipes create persistent child
requirements. The LLM sees the first unresolved prerequisite, current state,
available sites and public evidence, instead of the full ordinary actor history.
It proposes a site, recipe or container method. The program resolves exact
site/item references, supplies provenance and compiles one action. A site
method for missing material cannot redefine success as arrival. An enabling
object can be proposed without a prior successful use; its effect remains a
hypothesis to test. The model no longer has to generate evidence hashes or
predict the compiler's first action. An empty method list retains the actor.
No extra paid selector, game/entity-specific rule or private simulator is added.

After actual acquisition, the parent recipe resumes. Only observing the output
in hand establishes a held-output requirement. Unsupported routes/recipes and
unobserved effects remain unknown. The previous compiler's finite budgets and
resource checks remain. Exact registered aliases identify a task once; a truly
different registered task still reserves its own resources.
For an access-only fragment, the destination is just an attempted method.
Starting it requires the actor to be attempting that failed entrance; continuing
it requires the actor to still choose navigation after urgent-action handling. Object actions can supersede
it. This is a limited authority rule, not proof that the fallback action is good.

Frozen iterations and gate
e1 (172 tests): compact request and persistent task/recipe parent. No acquisition
or score improvement in five selected eight-action diagnostics; no full games.
e2 (174 tests): unify site methods and remove the contradictory keep-original
flag. Both Mark branches acquire, craft and hold the sword (+1 point); the
positive control is preserved. This passes the declared full-game gate.
e3 (176 tests): remove duplicate reservations only for exact aliases of one
registered task. Both Mark branches now finish in seven actions, each with one
failed craft that reveals the unobserved station requirement. This costs an
action; it is not silently counted as a successful craft or a speed improvement.
e4 (178 tests, final): cancel an access-only plan when the ordinary actor moves
on to non-navigation. Retain e1/e2/e3 artifacts and reuse their exact calls.
The final standard branch diagnostics exactly replay e3, making zero new calls.

Why the Mark-r1 whole-game gain is not stable
e2's ordinary disassemble-copper_sword at decision 83 recovers copper and rod;
its final score is 16. e3's decision 82 disassembly reports recovering nothing.
That material is needed for the later key task. The alias fix changed the
earlier trajectory and timing; the later states/RNG differ. The trace identifies
a decisive observed material difference, but does not identify a general causal
effect or expected benefit of the alias fix from one run. Early sword success
cannot substitute for preserving resources or finding a productive operation
for the subsequent task. No hidden future task or recovery result is supplied
to the policy. The ordinary actor's destructive action remains outside this
local repair mechanism's supervision.

Remaining gaps
1. A productive material-recovery method is still missing in the depleted state.
   Better reference grounding and successful navigation cannot invent one.
2. Public requirement extraction is incomplete, especially narrative tasks.
   Access fragments remain heuristic; the new yield rule only narrows their
   authority and does not solve global task-value estimation.
3. Long-horizon resource consequences and stochastic salvage are not covered
   by local compiler checks. Do not call the observed checks a safety theorem.
4. The local gains must survive independent worlds and repetitions before a
   robust game-performance or paper claim. This bounded development batch does
   not justify a costly formal evaluation or promoting the new default yet.
