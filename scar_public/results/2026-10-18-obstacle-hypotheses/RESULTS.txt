Bounded obstacle hypotheses: full development evaluation, 2026-10-18

Conclusion
The new repair layer can construct and execute a previously unverified remedy.
It fixes the two selected locked-armory states by acquiring the visible key
before retrying. It does not establish a stable whole-game score improvement.
Against the frozen core checked policy, wins/ties/losses: 1/2/1.
The opt-in arm is not promoted as the default.

Design and frozen iterations
h1 replaces the fixed repair menu with up to two LLM-proposed, source-grounded
but causally unverified programs of at most three actions. Existing scope checks
remain: public entity/action availability, inventory/capacity, declared/observed
resource spending, task reserves, and downstream failure conditions. Pickups
update the projected inventory before later edge checks. Every continuation
requires an actual public observation and a fresh check. Unknown is not failure.
h1 rejected a plausible key proposal because it falsely asserted possession.
h2 adds one bounded correction with rejection reasons and the actual current
state. This remains within the total 12 proposal-call budget. All four h1 games
are retained; h2 was separately frozen and evaluated on all four same worlds.

Matched local result
For both Mark worlds, h1 and h2 have identical private-state digests, original
actor actions and initial LLM proposals at decision 49. The initial h1 proposal
was reused exactly by payload/step cache, rather than sampled again. Correction
produced pickup(key), enter(armory). At 49 the pickup succeeded; at 50 the game
explicitly reported using the key to unlock the armory. The ordinary actor then
acquired copper, returned to the workbench, crafted and picked up the sword.
mark_r0: copper-sword task completed at h2 decision 54 versus checked decision 65.
mark_r1: copper-sword task completed at h2 decision 54 versus checked decision 68.
The key was not hard-coded. The proposer inferred a relation from public
observations (including prior key use at another entrance); only the current
state and proposed short program were checked. Two related Mark worlds do not
establish generalization or a 100-trial error reduction.

Failure diagnosis
Mark-r1 h2 still stalls at the next material bottleneck. The ordinary actor
already proposed disassembling the sword at decision 70 and called it reversible.
Actual feedback says it recovered nothing useful, and the sword disappeared.
The prior v2 success at decision 82 recovered one copper bar from disassembly.
Post-hoc evaluator inspection confirms the environment draws a 0.5 RNG test
for each recipe ingredient of quantity one and consumes the object regardless.
That rule was never supplied to the policy. These are distinct trajectory
states, not a paired causal comparison or evidence that the earlier repair
made the disassembly outcome worse. The new gate only supervises triggered
repairs; it does not globally supervise ordinary destructive actor actions.

Metropolis-r1 also illustrates limits: at decision 71 the alternate antechamber
entry succeeds where the original alley entry fails; at 124 both original
courtroom and replacement lobby entry fail. Feasible navigation and plausible
task_link text are insufficient to establish eventual task benefit. Likewise,
a later Mark-r1 library search validates arrival, not material acquisition.

Remaining gaps
1. Couple the final verification predicate to the actual proposed blocker
   change: an access hypothesis can test arrival, a material-source hypothesis
   needs evidence of the material, not just arrival.
2. Distinguish cost-bounded exploratory changes from irreversible experiments
   that can destroy the remaining useful resource. Apply that distinction to
   relevant ordinary actor decisions too, rather than only replacement actions.
3. Extend represented action/resource effects beyond pickup/navigation/dialogue/
   inspection before claiming support for general upstream material plans.
4. Freeze a stronger candidate and conduct independent repeated evaluation;
   these trajectories do not establish significance or stable improvement.
